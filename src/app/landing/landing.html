Atomic notes and the Zettelkasten method
one idea per note · link everything · write for your future self
the core idea
Each note should express one single idea completely. The power of Zettelkasten is not in individual notes but in the connections between them.

A note that links to 10 others is worth more than 10 isolated notes. The value is in the network, not the nodes.

writing principles
Write in your own words. Ask: what does this connect to? Write for your future self — assume zero context tomorrow.

what's inside

everything your brain needs,
on one desk

Built around one principle: notes should connect to everything, not sit in folders doing nothing.

An editor, not a text box

Tabbed markdown editor with wiki-links, callout blocks, slash commands, and live backlink tracking. Writes like code, thinks like prose.
core

Living knowledge graph

Nodes pulse and glow based on recency. Hot notes glow orange. Cold notes fade. See your active thinking — not a frozen snapshot.
signature

Native reading layer

Read epub, PDF, and articles in-app. Highlight passages, attach notes, capture directly into your vault — no clipboard, no app-switching.

Freeform canvas

Drag note cards anywhere. Connect them with arrows. Group related ideas. A spatial workspace for brainstorming — not a hierarchy.

Kanban + Todos

Tasks and todos in one smart split view. Every kanban card can link back to a note — so your work always connects to your thinking.

AI synthesis

Live suggestions in the context panel: missing links, synthesis opportunities, review reminders. AI that notices patterns, not a chatbot you have to prompt.

Study mode

For when the vault is a syllabus. An interactive tutor teaches a topic in steps, asks you questions back, and draws diagrams and timetables — all saveable into the note you are studying from.
for students

Daily journal

One zero-friction entry point every day. No decision about where to write — just open today. On This Day resurfaces past notes automatically.

Command palette first

⌘K for everything: jump to a note, run AI, search your vault, create a task. The UI stays out of the way — your keyboard runs the show.

Publish a note, or a whole shelf of them

Turn any note — or a folder and everything nested in it — into a link that works without an account. A published page is a snapshot, so your later edits stay private until you say otherwise, and links to notes you did not publish quietly stay plain text instead of becoming dead ends. Or take the whole vault out as markdown, whenever you want. It is your writing; the exit is not hidden.

how it works

capture → connect → create

Three stages, one loop. Most PKM apps break at the third stage. We're built around it.

01

Write without friction

Slash commands for any block type. Paste an article and keep its structure. Highlight passages from books. Whatever you learn, it goes in — fast.
02
connect

Ideas find each other

[[wiki-links]] create a living network. AI surfaces missing connections. The graph shows where your thinking is hot — and where it's going cold.
03
create

Your notes become things

Synthesize highlights into evergreen notes. Export as an essay. Map your reading onto a canvas. Your knowledge is a factory floor, not an archive.

reading layer

read, highlight, capture — without leaving

Import epub books, PDFs, and research papers. Clip articles and RSS feeds. Read them in-app with a clean focus view — and highlight passages that flow directly into your vault as atomic notes.

The floating toolbar appears on any selection: highlight in yellow, purple, or green. Attach a note. Link to an existing idea. Ask AI to explain the concept.

canvas

a corkboard for ideas — with no rules

Drag note cards anywhere on an infinite canvas. Connect related ideas with arrows. Group them with dashed regions. Drop sticky notes for fleeting thoughts that aren't full notes yet.

Different from the mind map — canvas is freeform. No forced hierarchy. You decide the shape of the thinking, not the app.

why this exists

a note is worth more for what it connects to

I kept starting note systems and abandoning them. The notes were fine. The problem was that nothing ever came back to me — I would write something good, file it in a folder, and never see it again.

So braindot is built around connection rather than storage. Notes link to each other, backlinks appear on their own, and the graph shows you the shape of what you have been thinking about. The AI can read across the whole vault, not one note at a time, which is what makes it able to notice things you have forgotten you wrote.

It is a working beta, built in the open. If you try it and something feels wrong, I would genuinely like to hear about it.
