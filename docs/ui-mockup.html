Braindot — UI mockup for a design pass

What this is. A static, pixel-faithful mockup of a personal knowledge-management app (local-first markdown notes, wiki-links, a knowledge graph, and an AI panel). Colours, spacing, type sizes and box metrics are copied verbatim from the live app — this is what it actually looks like today, not an idealised version.

The house style. Everything in the chrome is JetBrains Mono, lowercase labels, dark by default, 1px hairline borders, 4–8px radii, a single violet accent. Only the note body itself is serif. That constraint is deliberate and should stay — the ask is polish within it, not a restyle.

What I'd like critiqued:

  • Visual hierarchy — everything is currently the same weight; nothing draws the eye first.
  • Density and rhythm — is the 300px right panel too cramped for a chat? Is the dashboard too many panels of equal importance?
  • The dashboard specifically: it reads as a wall of cards. What should be hero, what should be secondary, what should be cut?
  • Colour discipline — violet/green/amber/blue/red all appear as semantic accents. Too many?
  • Empty and first-run states.
  • Anything that looks like a developer built it rather than a designer.

Writing is thinking

Not a metaphor
#craft #core +tag EVERGREEN

The claim is not that writing helps you think. It's that the thinking does not fully exist until it is written.

Evidence, weak but persuasive: everyone has had the experience of believing they understood something until they tried to write it down. The gap is not in the transcription. It is in the thought.

This is why [[Compression is understanding]] matters — if you can't compress it, you didn't have it. And it is why [[Zettelkasten, honestly]] is more useful as a writing practice than as a filing system.

Corollary: notes you never revisit are still worth writing. The value was extracted at the moment of writing. Everything after is interest.
