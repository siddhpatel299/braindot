Braindot — round 2: the four views round 1 didn't cover

Context. Round 1 covered the editor and dashboard, and its critique was implemented. These are the four remaining full-screen views: Kanban + Todos, Graph, Canvas, and Reading. Same app, same constraints. All four predate the round-1 pass, so they still carry the habits that pass identified.

Already decided in round 1 — please stay consistent with these, don't re-open them
Panel headings are sentence case, 12px/600, on --t1. Not uppercase micro-labels.
Legends were replaced by sentences. "Busiest in June — 9 events" beats a colour key nobody acts on.
Reading text is 16.5px/1.8 at ~66ch. Chrome sits at 10–13px. The content must outrank the frame.
Chips are text + a 5px dot. Filled backgrounds are reserved for the primary button and the active nav item.
Hierarchy over uniformity. The dashboard went from 11 equal panels to 5 ranked ones. Same medicine likely applies here.
Contrast floor is 4.5:1. --t3 (#444450) on panel background is 1.97:1 and was a real bug. It is still used as text below.
What I'd like critiqued
  • Kanban: is a 4-column board plus a todo rail two task systems where there should be one? The columns are fixed and unconfigurable.
  • Graph: the sidebar has three "color by" modes, a show filter, stats, an activity feed and a 14-day strip. Is any of it load-bearing, or is it a control panel for a toy?
  • Canvas: an infinite board in a PKM app — does it earn its place next to the graph, or are they the same feature twice?
  • Reading: three columns at once (library 220 / reader 915 / AI 250). The reader column is wide but the text measure inside is 680px, so ~235px of it is just margin — while both rails stay pinned open.
  • Across all four: every view invents its own header. Kanban stacks a 44px breadcrumb row on a 40px stat row — 84px of view chrome before the board, on top of the 48px global bar. Reading stacks a toolbar on a toolbar. Should there be one shared view-header pattern?
  • Empty states — none of these four have one.

The four underlying principles

How to Take Smart Notes · Sönke Ahrens

Writing is not what follows research, learning or studying, it is the medium of all this work. Seen this way, the question changes entirely.

Nobody ever starts from scratch. The blank page is a myth, and a paralysing one. Every text you write begins in the middle of a conversation you have already been having with yourself, in notes.

The slip-box is not an encyclopaedia. It is a thinking partner, and like any partner it becomes more useful the more honestly you engage with it. A note you cannot argue with is a note you did not need to write.

The consequence is uncomfortable: most note-taking advice optimises the wrong step. Capture is cheap. The expensive part — the part that produces understanding — is the reformulation.
